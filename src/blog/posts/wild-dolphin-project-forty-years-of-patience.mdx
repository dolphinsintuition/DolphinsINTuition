Most scientific breakthroughs in animal cognition happen fast. A lab study, a clever experiment, a result. Then the next paper.

The Wild Dolphin Project is different. Dr. Denise Herzing has been studying the same population of free-ranging Atlantic spotted dolphins in the Bahamas since 1984. Not their species. Not a rotating cast of individuals. *The same animals*, identified individually by their spot patterns, tracked year by year through their births, social bonds, deaths, and cultural transmission.

That's not a career choice. It's a scientific infrastructure decision — and it's why the WDP's dataset is irreplaceable.

## Why Longitudinal Data Changes Everything

When you study the same animals continuously for 40 years, things become visible that short-term studies structurally cannot see.

In 2013, a large group of immigrant Atlantic spotted dolphins entered the WDP's study area. Because the team had decades of baseline data on the resident population, they could measure exactly how the social network reorganised — which immigrants were accepted first, which residents were most open to association, how integration progressed over subsequent seasons. Published in *Marine Mammal Science* in 2023, this study is only possible because of the 30+ years of prior data it rests on.

The same logic applies to communication research. Signature whistles — the individually unique calls dolphins use to identify themselves — can only be reliably characterised across years of recorded vocalisations from known individuals. One field season gives you a sample. Forty field seasons give you a vocabulary.

## CHAT: Interaction, Not Translation

Since 2013, the Wild Dolphin Project has deployed CHAT (Cetacean Hearing and Telemetry) — a wearable computer system that plays sounds to dolphins in the water and records their responses.

The framing here is precise and important. CHAT is not a translation device. It's an interaction device. Researchers use it to test whether dolphins will respond to a specific sound consistently in a specific context — requesting an object, for example. In 2013, one dolphin was documented doing exactly that: associating a CHAT-played sound with a toy and producing that sound when it wanted the toy.

That's not language. But it's also not nothing. It's the beginning of a protocol — a way of testing whether meaning can be established across species.

The CHAT system has since been upgraded to run on Google Pixel phones, integrated with the DolphinGemma acoustic classifier developed in partnership with Google DeepMind. The field hardware is now more capable than it has ever been.

## Why This Matters for Independent Platforms

DolphinsIntuition is an independent research platform, not a marine biology institution. We can't run field seasons. What we can do is build tools that make this research more accessible — and that take the scientific framing seriously rather than overclaiming.

The WDP's "in their world, on their terms" principle is one we apply to how we talk about what AI can and cannot do with dolphin vocalisations. Classification is not translation. Interaction is not conversation. Those distinctions aren't limitations — they're the actual frontier, which is more interesting than the headlines suggest.
